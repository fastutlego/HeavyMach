# A developer's statement — Why I chose HeavyMach

<!-- language-nav:start -->
[한국어](../RESTORATION.md) | [English](RESTORATION.md)
<!-- language-nav:end -->

Someone named [mesabloo](https://github.com/Mesabloo/hm-defense) worked on a similar project. He obtained HeavyMach Defense assets and wrote its source code from scratch. He appears to have stopped while implementing combat between units. My starting point was better than his, since he had to write the source code from scratch.

One weekend, I had a dream. I saw myself from a third-person perspective, encountering the iPod touch and Cydia for the first time. After waking up, I searched Google for heavy Mach. Gemini in Google Search pointed me to [Internet Archive](https://archive.org), where I downloaded HeavyMach 1.13 from the [HeavyMach series archive](https://archive.org/details/heavy-mach-archive_202406) and began the restoration.

So I think I did very little. This is also why I feel I have even less reason to think of making money from it. In my own words, all I did was grab Codex by the collar and slap it whenever it arbitrarily departed from the original source code and its creators' artistic achievement and did something wrong.

Development was my third career. In 2015, I worked as a magazine journalist; in 2016, I worked in the Philippines. In July 2017, I switched careers to become an iOS developer. The world I experienced in jobs far removed from software felt nondeterministic and irrational to me. I had several reasons for leaving that life behind and choosing another profession. Over soju with friends, I sometimes hear that I would have ended my life by jumping to my death if I had continued doing what I did in 2015 and 2016. I still do not know whether they are right.

HeavyMach was also one of the reasons I left my life of 2015 and 2016 behind and chose another profession. I first encountered it on an iPod touch in college. After discovering it through Cydia, I later searched for instructions on creating a US Apple App Store account and bought it. The Korean App Store had no Games category at the time. I tilted the device to move the tank and fire, and Paperblue's illustrations appeared after each stage. Released in 2009, HeavyMach was the first game by a Korean developer to enter the App Store's top five paid apps. An interview with the developer at the time reported revenue of about KRW 140 million. [Interview from that period](https://www.joongang.co.kr/article/3534205)

I think HeavyMach accounted for about 35% of my decision to become a developer. That experience also helped me choose a Unity game based on HeavyMach's concept instead of copying Lineage, Counter-Strike, or later 3D games. (The Unity game is a separate project from this restoration of the original HeavyMach. This repository covers the original HeavyMach restoration.)

The events that had an even greater influence happened at the magazine. Editorial designers with skills that were difficult to replace collectively defied management, and their working conditions improved as a result. Except for the two days at the end of the monthly production deadline, they were guaranteed to leave work on time. I also wrote about this experience in SaaS's “The Price of Defiance.” [“The Price of Defiance” — Magazine V09](https://sass.studio/magazine/cerulean)

I also saw and heard things while interviewing executives at factory automation companies such as Rockwell Automation, B&R, and Yokogawa. They spoke about the benefits of automation through engineering. That reporting experience also influenced my choice to become a developer.

In 2026, I rebuilt the game that had shown me a direction in 2009. It is no longer on the App Store, and few people around me remember it. I wanted to run it again on the iPhone I use today. I had Codex write the source code anew and kept pointing out and correcting behavior that differed from the original.

Lately, I have felt that I do not have much time left. It is neither a health problem nor extreme lethargy. If anything, my mind feels clearer than it did a month, two months or half a year ago. So I think about what I want to try and what I want to leave behind. Restoring HeavyMach is one of those choices. A game that once showed me a direction, made to work again by me, now a developer. To me, this brings the end back to the beginning.

I do not know what the series' developer, Byun Hae-jun, and illustrator, Park Jae-cheol, think of this restoration project. Yesterday, I left an inquiry on a messenger profile that appears to belong to Park. I have not received a reply yet.

When I began the restoration, the creators I knew were Byun Hae-jun and Park Jae-cheol. That is why I recorded their names first. I knew of Mesabloo's restoration project, but I only learned that WeMade had acquired HeavyMach's IP while putting these credits together. [Creators and acquisition history](../../CREDITS.en.md)

Although it was not part of the original game, Ace Combat 8 gave me a reason to wait for its release and think that I should stay alive longer. By restoring HeavyMach and adding Lux Lunae to it, I put the roles these two works played in my life into one game I could play. [Why Lux Lunae?](public/lux-lunae-intent-20261028.md)

If I eventually create an App Store account for sass.studio and the original creators and stakeholders permit distribution, I will still offer it for free.

I have no intention of making money from this restoration. I want to publish the code and assets together so that other people can build and play it themselves. I also want to leave a record of how I restored the game and what I fixed. 
If a stakeholder contacts me, I will take this project down. The 9 years and 3 months that HeavyMach's creators gave me exceeded the lifespan my creator originally allotted to me. 

Money is only needed in this life.

## A developer's statement — Closing my final defense

After leaving my last company in 2025, I took up a new hobby.  
Writing death poems.  
I close my final defense as a developer with Hosokawa Gracia's death poem.

散りぬべき  
時知りてこそ   
世の中の   
花も花なれ  
人も人なれ

Only by knowing  
when it is time to fall  
in this world  
does a flower become a flower,  
and a person become a person.
